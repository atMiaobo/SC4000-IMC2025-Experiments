V23 Hypothesis: Use pycolmap.CameraMode.SINGLE instead of CameraMode.AUTO for video scenes in COLMAP feature extraction.

All prior versions V7–V22 call pycolmap.extract_features(..., camera_mode=pycolmap.CameraMode.AUTO), which assigns an independent camera model (SIMPLE_RADIAL by default) to every individual image. For forward-facing staircase video, all frames come from the same physical camera with a fixed focal length and principal point that do not change between frames. Under AUTO mode, COLMAP must estimate intrinsics separately for each image from the matches available at that point in the incremental reconstruction; for scenes of only 15–20 images with a sparse initial point cloud, these per-image estimates are poorly constrained and inconsistent across frames, producing depth ambiguities that reduce the number of well-triangulated 3D points visible in each candidate frame. V21 improved registration by lowering abs_pose_min_num_inliers from 4 to 3 (P3P minimum), allowing frames with only 3 matching 3D points to register; V22 added init_min_tri_angle=0.5 degrees and regressed (8→7 stairs cameras), so V23 reverts to V21's init_min_tri_angle=1.0. The new change is CameraMode.SINGLE: COLMAP shares one camera model across all images in the scene and jointly optimises its intrinsics during every bundle adjustment round. Joint optimisation across 15–20 frames provides far more constraints on focal length and principal point than any single seed pair, yielding a more accurately calibrated camera model and geometrically tighter 3D point positions. Tighter 3D points increase the fraction of adjacent frames that can accumulate the 3 inlier correspondences required for registration, directly attacking the bottleneck (scene_0: 3–5/15, scene_2: 3/20 registered cameras) that has limited stairs performance in all previous versions. Non-video scenes retain CameraMode.AUTO and V7 mapper settings, preserving ETs and hidden structured-capture dataset performance.
