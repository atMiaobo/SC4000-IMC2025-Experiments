V13 Hypothesis: The LGEU GBM model's num_gap feature is the root cause of poor pair
selection for video sequences like stairs. num_gap is computed as the minimum absolute
numeric difference between filename tokens divided by 1000; for 13-digit millisecond
Unix timestamps, two frames ~600 ms apart (typical at ~1.6 fps effective rate for 51
frames over ~30 s) produce num_gap ≈ 0.6. The GBM model associates high num_gap values
with cross-scene pairs (since same-scene non-video pairs often have small sequential
numeric indices), so it ranks these temporally distant video frame pairs with low
same-scene probability and excludes them from the TOPK=20 selection. This leaves
many video frames with no valid SIFT neighbors and strands them as outliers with zero
cameras registered — the failure mode observed in V9 (14 stairs outliers, 6/51 total).
V12 tried to patch this with temporal augmentation, but hurt scores (4/51) because
stairs has two simultaneous cameras whose timestamps interleave: adding ±2 sequential
temporal neighbors connects frames across camera streams, creating erroneous cross-stream
edges that collapse the two sub-scenes into one unresolvable cluster. V13 instead bypasses
the LGEU model entirely for video sequences and ranks pairs by raw cosine similarity of
global descriptors (top-20 per image). Cosine similarity captures visual appearance
directly without the timestamp artifact, is the natural distance metric the global
descriptor was trained under, and is robust to the dual-camera interleaved-timestamp
structure because it scores pairs by what they look like, not when they were captured.
Non-video datasets (ETs and all hidden structured-capture datasets) are unaffected and
use the standard V9 GBM+TOPK=20 path. We expect fewer outlier images for stairs and
a net increase in registered cameras over V9 (25/73) without regressing ETs (19/22).
